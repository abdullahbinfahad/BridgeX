# BridgeX Competition Project Plan — Executive Review

## Overall assessment

The submitted Chinese project plan is **substantially stronger than an ordinary idea-stage proposal**. It describes the user problem, limits the product claim to a coordination platform rather than a carrier, avoids invented traction, and repeatedly states that cross-border expansion depends on legal and operational validation. Those choices make the plan more credible for an undergraduate innovation competition.

The plan should not be called “perfect” yet. Its greatest opportunity is to become more evidence-led: the next version needs real user-research evidence, a narrowly defined pilot, a clearer unit-economics hypothesis, an explicit risk register, and a precise distinction between implemented functions, prototype functions, and future roadmap items.

| Area | Current quality | Why it works | Main gap | Priority action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Problem definition | Strong | Identifies fragmented trust, coordination, and capacity-discovery problems | Needs first-hand interview or survey evidence | Add a documented research appendix |
| Positioning | Strong | Correctly states that BridgeX is not itself the carrier | Needs a narrower first pilot | Choose one legal community and route type |
| Product workflow | Good | Covers posting, matching, private communication, verification, and order states | Some workflow claims need prototype screenshots/test evidence | Add annotated evidence map |
| Compliance | Strong for an idea-stage plan | Avoids promises of customs clearance, insurance, or fund custody | Requires a formal risk-control matrix | Add prohibited-item, escalation, and retention policy |
| Market analysis | Careful but incomplete | Correctly labels adjacent market data as non-equivalent | No bottom-up pilot market sizing | Add TAM/SAM/SOM assumptions and validation plan |
| Business model | Incomplete | Mentions service coordination direction | Needs pricing assumptions and cost model | Add unit-economics hypothesis, not revenue promises |
| Team execution | Incomplete | Shows technical prototype direction | Needs role ownership and cadence | Attach the task sheet and responsibility matrix |

## What should remain exactly as it is

The proposal should retain its legal caution. It should continue to say that platform rules do not replace users’ obligations, and that the platform does not promise customs clearance, transport legality, payment custody, insurance, or universal route availability. This is more defensible than overstating the platform’s role.

The proposal is also right not to present adjacent delivery-market growth as BridgeX revenue, order volume, or market share. The cited market figures can support a context section, but the project should clearly separate external market trends from its own unproven demand.

## Critical changes before submission or pitch

### 1. Add a pilot-validation appendix

The competition team should collect first-party evidence. A useful minimum is 30–50 structured survey responses plus 8–12 interviews from the intended campus or international-student community. Record the research date, participant profile, consent method, question list, raw counts, and limitations. Do not use fake feedback, fake reviews, or invented users.

Suggested questions are: how often a participant needs to send small legal goods or documents; current alternatives; route and time requirements; expected trust controls; willingness to post a request or capacity; and reasons they would not use the product.

### 2. Define a single lawful pilot

The current plan spans domestic and cross-border scenarios. For a competition proposal, select one starting scenario, such as verified campus members coordinating lawful document or personal-item handoff within a defined domestic region. Treat other scenarios as a future expansion hypothesis.

### 3. Add evidence that the product exists

Include a concise prototype evidence table. Each claimed feature should be classified as **implemented**, **demo-ready**, **in development**, or **planned**. Pair implemented claims with screenshots, a recorded walkthrough, source-control date, and a short test statement. Do not claim a production payment, insurance, or compliance integration where one does not exist.

### 4. Add a unit-economics hypothesis

Use a simple model instead of an unsupported valuation claim.

| Variable | Definition | Pilot measurement method |
|---|---|---|
| Qualified post rate | Share of posts that meet safety and completeness checks | Review a weekly sample |
| Match rate | Matched requests divided by eligible requests | Product analytics |
| Completion rate | Completed orders divided by matched orders | Order-status data |
| Support cost | Team time per active order or incident | Weekly operations log |
| Trust cost | Verification, moderation, storage, and dispute-handling time | Activity ledger |
| Take-rate hypothesis | A future service-fee assumption, subject to legal review | Never call it revenue until payments are lawful and operational |

### 5. Add a formal risk register

| Risk | Early control | Escalation owner | Evidence to retain |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prohibited or misdeclared item | Mandatory declaration, reporting, removal workflow | Safety administrator | Post record, decision reason, timestamps |
| Identity misuse | Secure verification queue and role-limited review | Verification administrator | Submission status; not documents in public exports |
| Payment dispute | Evidence status and clear non-custody wording | Payment reviewer | Proof metadata, decision log |
| Data exposure | RLS policies, signed URLs, least privilege | Technical lead | Audit log, access review |
| Route or delivery failure | Clear status updates and dispute procedure | Operations lead | Order timeline and messages |

## Competition-pitch structure

The clearest oral pitch is: **problem → narrow pilot → product evidence → trust controls → measured validation → expansion criteria**. It is more persuasive to say “we will validate this route and this community with these measurements” than to claim immediate global scale.

## Reference integrity

Keep the cited market links in the proposal, but audit them before submission because publisher pages and forecast editions can change. Also cite primary official sources when describing regulation, customs, aviation, privacy, or payment requirements; marketing pages are not legal authority.

## Review conclusion

The plan is competition-ready after a disciplined evidence appendix and a narrower pilot. Its most valuable characteristic is honesty about the platform’s current stage. The team should protect that credibility by validating claims, documenting limitations, and separating prototype capability from future commercial capability.
